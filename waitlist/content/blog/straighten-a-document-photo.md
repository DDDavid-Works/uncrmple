You photographed a form, a receipt or a letter, and the page came out as a trapezoid: wide at the bottom, narrow at the top, and leaning to one side. The text is all there, but the photo looks nothing like a scan, and it prints badly.

The good news is that a slanted page is one of the easiest problems to fix, as long as all four corners of the page are in the photo.

## Why the page looks slanted

A camera only gives you a flat, square-on picture of a page when the lens points straight down at its centre. Hold the phone at even a small angle and the near edge of the page is closer to the lens than the far edge, so it comes out larger. The rectangle of the page turns into a four-sided shape with no right angles.

This is called perspective distortion. It is not blur, and no information is lost: every line of text is still in the photo, just stretched by different amounts in different places.

## How perspective correction works

Because a sheet of paper is a flat rectangle, the distortion follows a precise rule. If you know where the four corners of the page are in the photo, you can calculate how every point on the page was stretched, and reverse it.

Repair tools do this in three steps:

1. **Find the page.** The tool looks for the edges and corners of the sheet against its background.
2. **Choose the output shape.** It sizes the flat page from the lengths of its sides in the photo, so the page comes out close to its real proportions rather than squashed.
3. **Warp the photo.** Every pixel is moved to where it would be if the photo had been taken from straight above, and the background outside the page is cropped away.

The result is a flat, upright page that reads like a scan.

## When automatic detection misses

Finding the page is the hard part. It works best when the paper stands out from what is behind it. It struggles when:

- the page is white on a white or pale table,
- part of the page is covered by a hand, a clip or another sheet,
- a corner is cut off by the edge of the photo,
- the page is folded or curled so its edges are not straight.

When this happens, a good tool lets you drag the four corners onto the page yourself. That takes a few seconds and gives exactly the same correction, because the maths only needs the four corners.

If a corner is outside the photo, no tool can bring it back. The best you can do is crop what is there, so it is worth checking for all four corners before you leave the document behind.

## How to take a photo that needs less fixing

- **Get all four corners in the frame**, with a little background around the page.
- **Use a contrasting background.** A dark table or a sheet of coloured card behind white paper makes the edges easy to find.
- **Hold the phone flat over the page**, roughly parallel to it. A slight angle is fine; a steep one stretches the far end of the page so much that small text loses detail.
- **Flatten the paper** if you can. Straightening fixes the angle of a flat sheet, but creases and curls bend the lines of text inside it.
- **Keep it sharp.** Tap to focus and hold still. Straightening cannot undo blur.
